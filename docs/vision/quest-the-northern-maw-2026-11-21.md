# Dungeons & Crows: The Northern Maw
## Polar waterfall expedition, wooden longship, and morphic Agartha entrance

**Status:** Concept / proposed playable quest, not implemented  
**Date:** 2026-11-21  
**Continuity:** One connected Dungeons & Crows open-world universe; the Agartha-like interior is a fictional region reachable through multiple routes.

### Creative premise
An old mariner reports an ocean waterfall at the northern end of the known world. Navigators who steer toward the auroral storms report spun compasses, shifting stars, mysterious voices, and fleets that return with decks coated in salt from an ocean nobody has charted. A wooden, Viking-inspired longship is the player's first realistic chance to attempt the route: sturdy, reparable, light enough for oars, and reliant on analog navigation rather than electronic systems. **A wooden hull is not magically immune to magnetic fields**; in the game's fictional rules its principal advantage is avoiding failure of modern or enchantment-sensitive navigation equipment.

### A playable expedition: The Northern Maw

**Phase 1: Whispers of the Maw.** In a coastal settlement, compare incompatible sailors' maps. Quest clues are spread among a shipwright, a survivor, a crow's-eye view of the aurora, and an ancient stone marker. The Dungeon Master can reorder clues but cannot remove every clue needed to solve the route.

**Phase 2: Build and choose.** Assemble a Viking-inspired longship and crew. Choose reinforced hull, reserve oars, ballast, sails, lines, and provisions. At least two different build strategies must be viable; not a hidden one-correct-item puzzle. Optional crow scouting reveals hazards.

**Phase 3: The False Horizon.** As the ship nears the fictional polar opening, the sea inclines inward and the expected horizon rises into the air. Instruments behave erratically under the fantasy anomaly. Ocean spray turns into a ghosted mirage; some apparent rocks are safe channels and others are genuine obstacles. Navigation cues include sound, spray direction, nearby birds, and visible shoreline geometry, not only visual distortion.

**Phase 4: Ride the Fall.** The ship reaches a massive ring of falling ocean around the inner rim. The crew can cut sail, row across crosscurrents, anchor to a floating basalt chain, or deploy the player's earned wings to scout the drop. The sequence shifts from ocean surface to near-vertical river to an interior spiral. Gravity and camera orientation rotate together, with an adjustable comfort option.

**Phase 5: The Decision Chamber.** At the inflection zone, three routes appear:
- **Follow the water:** fast and dangerous, surfaces into the inner ocean and its leviathan reefs.
- **Enter the stone throat:** slower, reveals ancient machinery, hidden loot, and a ground-based arrival.
- **Follow the spectral fleet:** high-risk morphic route through echoing duplicate ships and a lost naval battle, with an unusual character-memory reward.

**Phase 6: The Other Side.** The ship emerges into a colossal interior basin where the far continents visibly curve upward. Players see the small fictional inner sun and reachable distant coastlines. A successful landing opens a persistent map route and a new harbor. A failed attempt can strand the crew in a salvage area, trigger a rescue expedition, or force retreat; it does not erase permanent progression.

### Nightmare / hallucinatory mission variants
These are *in-game surreal visual and narrative effects*, not claims about real perception or real physics:
1. **The Sea Remembers:** crew hear a battle that has not happened; their choices can prevent, cause, or redirect it.
2. **A Ship With No Wake:** an identical longship crosses against the current, carrying alternate versions of the crew; interact or avoid.
3. **The Falling Sky:** for a short designed encounter, the ocean becomes the sky and enemies fight on both sides of a rotating gravity plane.
4. **The Oar of Lost Names:** a relic reveals a secret passage, but only if the player solves a navigational riddle.
5. **The Crow's Impossible Compass:** a flock forms shifting arrows; players must judge which clue is reliable from observed world rules.

### Design rules for fairness and accessibility
- **Player agency:** attempt, retreat, repair, return, or take a different route later.
- **Bounded randomness:** Dungeon Master may remix weather, enemies, hazards, and loot, never make the passage unknowably impossible.
- **Readable distortions:** distinct audio and visual cues separate safe from unsafe surfaces; player can reduce camera roll, flashes, and disorienting effects.
- **No softlocks:** after a failed descent there is a checkpoint, recoverable boat, rescue, or return route.
- **Physics contract:** spherical world coordinates, transition corridor, rotating local gravity vector, water-velocity fields, streaming chunks, and save-safe world-state changes. Large panoramas are distinct from collision/navmesh geometry.
- **Multiplayer extension:** party ship and environment states are authoritative and synchronized.
- **Scientific distinction:** real Earth has no established polar hole into Agartha; Earth's magnetic field does not normally repel airplanes, satellites, or wooden boats. These are fictional setting mechanics.

### First vertical slice and acceptance tests
Build only a limited instance initially:
1. A controllable boat approaches a visible curved polar waterfall.
2. A reversible transition smoothly rotates gravity and camera framing.
3. Three marked navigational choices result in different arrival locations.
4. At least one morphic encounter changes environmental geometry or enemy behavior rather than only screen effects.
5. Boat and character state, inventory, and checkpoint survive entry, exit, and reload.
6. Failed attempts offer retreat/retry without losing protected permanent progression.
7. No claim of a complete Earth-scale globe or a released playable system until tested.
