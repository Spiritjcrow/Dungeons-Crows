## Pilot: *The World Curves Up*

**Audience:** Fantasy RPG players, worldbuilding enthusiasts, independent game developers, and people interested in unusual simulated worlds.  
**Promise:** A clear introduction to a fictional game setting and the actual player experience it could create, not proof of an alternative physical Earth model.  
**Core idea:** The horizon seems to climb above the player; destinations glimpsed across the inner shell later become reachable; war and gravity events transform how exploration works.

### 75-second teaser script

> Imagine a world where the horizon doesn't fall away. It climbs.
>
> Across the sky, entire coastlines curve above you. An ocean stretches along the ceiling of the world. Somewhere beyond the clouds, another civilization can look down and see your kingdom.
>
> You begin on horseback, crossing fields beneath a small inner sun. Then the Dungeon Master changes the rules. The river rises. A distant battle lifts into the air. Mountains move, not because the game loaded another map, but because the world itself has entered a new state.
>
> Your character turns translucent. In Ghost-Pause, you inspect your armor, change class, and return to the fight with a different set of abilities. Eventually, black wings open paths that once seemed impossible to reach.
>
> This is the proposed world of **Dungeons & Crows**: an open-world action RPG about discovery, transformation, and wars fought across an impossible inner horizon.
>
> The vision is real as a design. The challenge now is making it genuinely playable.

### Visual shot sequence

1. **0–12 s:** Character on hill; land visibly curves upward into the far sky.
2. **12–25 s:** Sweep across an overhead ocean and illuminated distant settlements.
3. **25–38 s:** Horseback travel; landscape begins feathering and folding into a different state.
4. **38–52 s:** Battlefield gravity anomaly; armies and siege equipment drift upward.

### Outline for a 12-minute seminar

- **0:00–1:30:** The creative problem: making exploration surprising without losing player orientation.
- **1:30–4:00:** Inner-world geography, readable horizons, overhead seas, and scale cues.
- **4:00–6:00:** Strange-gravity gameplay and how movement can remain controllable.
- **6:00–8:00:** Morphic world states, navigation, and reversible transitions.
- **8:00–10:00:** Dungeon Master agency with deterministic protections and meaningful choices.
- **10:00–12:00:** A practical playable vertical slice and what would count as evidence that it works.

## Practical, no-budget publishing path

1. Preserve a private, timestamped transcript of each selected discussion, noting the original context.
2. Edit a public script that omits private family, account, location, and health details not relevant to the game.
3. Use existing concept art with clear **concept visualization** labels. Do not call it gameplay capture.
4. Record a fresh narration or select authorized voice material after checking the relevant permissions.
5. Publish one short teaser and one deeper explainer using free distribution; assess audience retention and questions before producing a larger series.
6. Keep a project provenance ledger: concept date, source conversation, author/editor, images used, claims, and production status.

## Commercial and accuracy boundaries

- **Originality:** Inward-curving / hollow fictional worlds have precedents, including *InnerSpace* (2018). Position the **combination of systems** and implementation vision as the differentiator rather than claiming no one has ever depicted an inverted world.
- **Science:** The inner sun, polar openings, ring accretion, and Schumann-driven landscape genesis are worldbuilding hypotheses for fiction, not established facts about Earth. The user mentioned an approximately 2.7-Hz frequency; do not assert this as a measured fundamental Schumann resonance or as evidence of terrain formation.
- **Readiness:** Mark all unrealized systems as designs/proposals until real engine tests show them working. Older unrelated prototypes are not proof that this complete vision is implemented.
- **Rights and privacy:** Do not make a voice-chat recording public, impersonate a participant, upload original audio, or sell conversation material without a separate deliberate review and explicit publication approval.
- **Revenue:** No earnings are guaranteed. The immediate target is evidence of public interest and a useful audience, not paid subscriptions or upfront production expenses.
